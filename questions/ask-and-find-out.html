      Why it works this way

      • Each puzzle tells him some things and hides one. He has to notice what he doesn't know and ask for it. Asking for something he already knows gets a giggle and “we know that already!”.
      • The answer is the reward: the box opens, the friend is found, the scene plays out. That is what makes asking carry over to real life.
      • Seeing means knowing: Ollie kept his eyes open (or saw in the dark), so Ollie is the one to ask. Understanding who knows things is what makes someone worth asking.
      • Built on Pivotal Response Treatment: he chooses (the world, the box, the picture), the tasks vary, easy ones come back, every try gets a reaction.
      • The real-life mission matters most: when he asks you something, answer warmly.

      Stars are never taken away. One star for each question that finds out what was missing on the first go, one for using the reason right first time, one per “I wonder…” picture, and one a day for the real-life mission. The Discovery Book is kept too.

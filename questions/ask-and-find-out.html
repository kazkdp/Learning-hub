      Why it works this way

      • Each puzzle tells him some things and hides one. He has to notice what he doesn't know and ask for it. Asking for something he already knows gets a kind “we know that already!”.
      • The answer is the reward: the box opens, the cat is found. Research on teaching autistic children to ask questions found this is what makes asking carry over to real life.
      • Question words come in the usual order: What, Where, Who, then Why. Help fades from the whole question, to the word, to a clue, to nothing.
      • The real-life mission is the most important part: ask him to ask you something, and answer warmly.

      Stars are never taken away. One star for each question that finds out what was missing on the first go, one for using the reason right first time, one per picture in “I wonder…”, and one a day for the real-life mission.

      How it works

      • It follows Colourful Semantics, the way many UK schools and speech and language therapists teach sentences: Who? (orange), What doing? (yellow), What? (green), Where? (blue). Joining words are pink and reasons purple.
      • Each stage adds one new thing. Inside a stage, help fades step by step: coloured cards and boxes with pictures → coloured boxes only → plain cards with one card that doesn't match the picture.
      • The full stop is a card of its own, so he learns that it always goes last. Questions end with a question mark; the detective stage finds missing full stops and capital letters.
      • Finding his level: when he gets the first 2 at a step right first time, he is offered ⬆️ challenge sentences from up to 2 stages ahead. Three right in a row and he moves up there (just below the top of that stage); a missed challenge costs nothing. If the jump was too big (3 misses before 5 right), he goes back to the first stage he skipped, and stages he skipped come back as “remember this one?” sentences until he has shown them.
      • Moving on needs about 80%: at the hardest step of a stage, 8 of his last 10 right first time (or his first 5 there all right, so a confident child moves on quickly). Two misses in a row make it a step easier. Every visit starts with 2 easy warm-ups, and every 4 sentences one from an earlier stage comes back.
      • Mistakes are gentle: the card wobbles back and a tip says what to look at. After a few tries the right card glows, so he never gets stuck.
      • When a sentence is finished it is read back once, then the next one comes by itself after a moment (or tap Next). There are no extra facts after each sentence, so all his attention stays on building it.

      Stars are never taken away. One star for each sentence built (or fixed) right first time.
